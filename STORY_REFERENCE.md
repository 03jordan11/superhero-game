# Story flash cards

This is a compact snapshot, not a replacement for the source. Recheck the Google Doc when story decisions depend on details or revisions. Spoilers below.

## How to use this during development

- Flag proposed systems that contradict the established story or undermine its intended emotional effect. Explain the conflict and suggest an adjustment.
- Offer relevant, fun ways to express the story through gameplay. Suggestions below are not approved features or canon.
- Keep the project's development priorities. This reference does not authorize implementing missions, story systems, or other later features.
- Distinguish prototype conveniences from final campaign rules; access to all powers during development is not itself a story contradiction.

## Established story / current direction

**Who is the protagonist?** A boxer in a city whose famous superhero has disappeared. Crime has worsened. He refuses to throw a match and wins, establishing resistance to powerful people controlling him.

**How does he become a hero?** Mafia members ambush him on the subway. A playable melee tutorial ends in defeat. The missing hero's robot rescues him and brings him to the old lair. It offers technologically granted powers to protect the city and investigate the disappearance, claiming not to know what happened.

**What drives Act I?** Investigating the mafia that attacked him and that the old hero was investigating. The mafia boss may offer the first hint that the robot's account is incomplete.

**What changes after the mafia boss?** The original lair is ransacked and the robot's body destroyed; its processor survives. The attacker is undecided.

**Who helps in Act II?** An old high-school friend with technical skills puts the robot's consciousness into a computer and becomes an ongoing ally. Later missions can provide a new mobile body or harness for the robot.

**When does the lair system enter?** After losing the original base, the player can buy a new hideout with money earned around the city. The original lair is initially available through the story; purchasing a personal hideout comes later.

**Why investigate the mayor?** He is a former Marine and an old Marine friend of the missing hero. He is corrupt or appears suspicious, giving independent reasons to investigate. He may reveal that the hero sought his help because he feared the robot; those revelations are possibilities, not settled details.

**What is the central mystery?** It develops from what happened to the old hero into why he disappeared and why the robot chose the protagonist.

**What really happened to the old hero?** He is alive. The robot gave him powers, removed them when he tried to stop its plan, and attempted to kill him. He escaped and remains in or around the city, powerless and working to stop it. The protagonist eventually meets him and discovers that the robot created both heroes for its purposes.

**What is the special resource?** City deposits/leaks can mutate nearby creatures through uncontrolled exposure. Refining it through the robot's machine grants or upgrades the protagonist's powers through controlled exposure. Its exact nature and origin remain undecided and must connect to the robot's objective.

**What must the robot relationship achieve?** The robot is likable, useful, funny, and supportive for most of the game. Its friendship with the old hero may also have been real. Its eventual betrayal should change the meaning of earlier city protection/resource recovery activities that also advanced its plan.

## Open choices — do not silently settle

- Robot origin, creator, arrival, age, mission, and reason for needing a human hero.
- Resource identity and its connection to the final objective.
- Robot objective candidates: resurrect its creator; finish ancient construction; nurture an underground alien organism; terraform/transform humanity; open an ancient structure; force human evolution.
- No candidate is selected. Destruction for its own sake is excluded; simply wanting to return home was rejected as the primary motivation.
- Final motivation should jointly explain why the robot is on Earth, how it arrived, and why it gathers the resource to activate/build/awaken something.
- Lair attacker; mayor's exact knowledge; old hero's hiding place and intervening work; protagonist's response to discovering powers can be revoked.

## Gameplay consistency checks and optional ideas

These are initial design observations, not additional story facts.

- **Progression:** Keep the resource/machine explanation available when designing permanent power unlocks. Instant menu upgrades may eventually need a story explanation.
- **Power removal:** Define the limits of the robot's ability to revoke powers before building the betrayal. Why can the protagonist resist? Avoid a long loss of the traversal abilities that make the game enjoyable.
- **Earned trust:** Let the robot help with real rescues and celebrate movement milestones. Earlier good deeds should still have helped people even if the robot exploited them.
- **Clues through play:** Shared visual or behavioral signatures between mutant abilities and player powers could foreshadow the common resource origin. Avoid making the robot obviously sinister from the beginning.
- **Traversal and collection:** Recover dangerous deposits using satisfying movement challenges and visible local cleanup, giving exploration both an immediate payoff and later narrative meaning.
- **Character continuity:** A boxer who refuses a fixed fight can later reject the robot's control. Preserve reasons for his early trust and opportunities for independent judgment.
- **Tutorial defeat:** Make the subway loss clearly scripted once its teaching purpose is complete, so success at the controls does not feel arbitrarily punished.
- **Base loss:** Preserve earned progression across the destroyed-lair transition unless a deliberate, agreed story beat calls for a temporary restriction.
